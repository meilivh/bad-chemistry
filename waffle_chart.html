Europe overview

Three countries account for half of all chemical sector emissions

Three countries, the Netherlands (67 MtCO₂e), Germany (61 MtCO₂e), and France (46 MtCO₂e), together account for half of all EU chemical sector emissions since the beginning of Phase 4. This reflects the historical geography of European industrial chemistry, where production clusters around port infrastructure, feedstock access, and integrated pipeline networks.

City-level focus

Antwerp, Sittard-Geleen, Ludwigshafen — three cities at the heart of European chemistry

At the city level, the picture sharpens further. Antwerp's chemical installations alone emit more than Italy's entire chemical sector. It hosts 16 chemical installations belonging to several companies, including BASF, Air Liquide, TotalEnergies, and Evonik.

Sittard-Geleen hosts one installation owned by the Saudi Arabian Oil Company that emits almost as much as all Czech chemical installations together.

Ludwigshafen is home to BASF's main production site and is also the second city to receive the most free allowances. The German city hosts 43 different installations, 42 of which are owned by BASF. Installations in that hub have received more free allowances than they have emitted since the beginning of Phase 4. Several of the most overallocated subsectors are also among those most likely to benefit from the layered allocation rules around waste gas heat recovery.
